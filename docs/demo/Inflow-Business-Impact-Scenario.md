# Inflow business impact scenario

Illustrative assumptions, not measured results or validated targets.

| Input | Assumed value |
|---|---:|
| Comparable eligible incidents per month | 1,000 |
| Current mean preparation effort per incident | 15 minutes |
| Mean preparation effort with Inflow, including review and edits | 10 minutes |

Preparation difference: 15 − 10 = **5 minutes per incident**.

Relative effort reduction: 5 ÷ 15 × 100 = **33.3%**, rounded to 33% on the slide.

Monthly released engineering capacity: 5 × 1,000 ÷ 60 = **83.3 hours**, rounded to 83 hours on the slide. This is equivalent to 10.4 eight-hour working days, not a staffing reduction or a claim of realized savings.

Use measured pilot means for aggregate capacity calculations. Keep median, sample sizes and variability in the evaluation report. Include time spent checking and correcting proposals in assisted effort. Use comparable incident categories and priorities in each cohort.

For financial evaluation: released capacity × agreed loaded hourly cost − model and allocated infrastructure costs. Do not report that capacity value as cash savings unless the business actually avoids those costs.

Measure suggestion usefulness, end-to-end resolution time and seven-day reopen rate alongside effort. Lower preparation time alone does not establish successful or faster resolution. The companion business measurement plan defines the data fields and scorecard.

## Presenter wording

“To show what the benefit could mean, take a team handling 1,000 eligible incidents a month. If preparation falls from 15 to 10 minutes, including review, that is 33% less preparation effort and about 83 engineer-hours available for other work each month. This is an illustrative scenario. Our pilot will measure the actual benefit.”
